# Agricultural Market Resilience in Germany

*A Comparative Analysis of Wheat, Barley and Potatoes Markets*

# Project Overview

Problem
Agricultural markets are exposed to external shocks that can disrupt supply chains, increase price volatility, and affect domestic production and trade. However, the extent to which agricultural markets in Germany are affected by such shocks, how they adjust, and how quickly they recover may differ across commodities. A comparison of wheat, barley, and potatoes can help identify differences in market vulnerability and resilience.

Goal
Analyze the mechanisms of selected agricultural markets in Germany to external crisis shocks, focusing on wheat, barley, and potatoes, in order to answer the following questions:

How strongly do prices respond to external crisis shocks, and how do price adjustment patterns differ across the three commodities?
How do production, imports, exports, and available stocks change during crisis periods?
How do the three agricultural markets differ in their vulnerability to external shocks and their capacity to adapt?
How quickly do prices and supply conditions recover toward pre-crisis levels?

Approach
Comparative analysis of wheat, barley, and potato markets across pre-crisis, crisis, adjustment, and recovery phases. The analysis focuses on price dynamics, supply indicators, and international trade dependencies to assess shock magnitude, response timing, shock duration, and recovery speed.
